Tax and buying power at each salary level

Why close to 90% between {{ trapText[0] }} and {{ trapText[1] }}? Three low-wage discounts shrink as your pay rises: your employer's structural reduction (up to 30 cents per extra €), your social work bonus (27–32 cents per extra €) and the tax credit tied to that bonus. So each extra euro pays the normal taxes and costs you part of those discounts. Above {{ trapText[1] }} they're used up and the rate falls back to about 70%.
Why the step at {{ topText[0] }}{{ topText[1] ? ' and the small dip at ' + topText[1] : '' }}? From {{ topText[0] }} your net taxable income passes {{ eur(P.brackets[2][0]) }}, so the top 50% income tax bracket starts, and the communal tax on it grows too.
Rules used · income year 2026 (assessment year 2027)

1 · Employer social security

  • Base rate 25.00%: 19.88% basic + 5.12% wage moderation.
  • Small funds: closure fund 0.32% (under 20 workers) or 0.37%, temporary unemployment 0.09%, risk groups 0.10%, asbestos fund 0.01% for quarters 1–3 only.
  • Structural reduction for low wages, per quarter: 0.14×(11 687.74 − S) + 0.16×(9 738.14 − S), where S is the quarterly wage and each term counts only when positive.
  • White-collar double holiday pay (0.92 × monthly) is paid without employer contributions.

2 · Employee social security

  • 13.07% of gross. On double holiday pay, only the part up to 85% of monthly pay is subject; the other 7% is exempt.
  • Social work bonus (per month, reference wage S): part A 127.54 − 0.3196×(S − 2 937.93), zero above €3 336.98. Part B 171.99 − 0.2699×(S − 2 300.62), zero above €2 937.93.
  • Special social security contribution: capped at €731.28/yr for singles. Couples use separate scales (max €619.68 when both partners earn).

3 · Taxable income

  • Gross − employee social security = taxable pay.
  • Flat-rate professional expenses: 30%, capped at €6 070.
  • Tax brackets:

4 · Tax-free allowance & children

  • Base allowance €11 550, raised by the 2026 reform from €11 180. It keeps rising toward €15 600 by 2030.
  • The allowance is taxed at the same brackets and that amount is credited against your tax. It is not a 0% slice of income.
  • Supplements for 1/2/3/4 children: €2 130 / 5 130 / 11 440 / 18 510, plus €7 070 for each extra child and €740 per child under 3.
  • Single with children: +€1 980, applied automatically. Unused child allowance can offset your tax (up to €550 per child), but only down to zero.

5 · Couples (joint return)

  • Each partner is taxed in their own column, with their own allowance. The totals are added.
  • Marital quotient: if one partner earns less than 30% of the couple's combined professional income, income moves across until they reach 30%, at most €11 780.
  • Child supplements go to the partner with the higher income. An allowance a partner can't use moves to the other partner.
  • Here, your tax = the couple's total tax − what the couple would pay if you earned nothing, never below zero.

6 · Federal, regional and communal tax

  • The brackets give the basic tax. The federal state keeps 75.043% of it. The region gets the rest (autonomy factor 24.957%) and adds its own surcharge on the federal part: 33.257% in Flanders and Wallonia, 32.591% in Brussels.
  • Communal tax = (federal + regional tax) × your commune's rate. The rates for all 565 communes come from the FPS Finance list for assessment year 2026, the latest one published.
  • Tax work bonus credit: 33.14% of part A plus 52.54% of part B of the social work bonus.

7 · VAT on spending

  • Standard rate 21%, applied to all of your net pay as if you spent it. Use the slider to see other rates.
  • VAT inside a price = price × r / (100 + r): at 21% that's 21 / 121 = 17.36% of what you pay.

What's not included

  • Benefits in kind (meal vouchers, company car, group insurance), personal deductions (pension savings, service vouchers, gifts), excise duties and blue-collar holiday-fund rules.
  • Figures are your final annual tax, not the monthly withholding, which is only an advance on it.
