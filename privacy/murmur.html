Murmur privacy policy

Last updated: 4 November 2026

Murmur plays your music, sends this device’s sound or songs to a friend on your local network, and, when you turn receiving on, lets friends play on this device. A friend is another device running Murmur, or another receiver such as Voicemeeter. There is no Murmur server, no account and no sign-up.

This policy describes what the app does. Where it says “we”, that means Aukmind, who publish Murmur.

What we collect

Nothing.We operate no server, no accounts and no analytics.

There is no advertising, no tracking, no telemetry and no crash reporting. Murmur talks only to devices on your network, and we never receive anything from it.

What stays on your device

  • Your identity. Key pairs generated on the device: one friends use to recognise this device when it sends or controls, one they use to recognise it as a speaker. They are not tied to you or your accounts. Settings can replace the sending one, after which friends see a new device.
  • Names. The name you send under and the name this device receives under, by default the device model.
  • Saved friends: their names, addresses and keys, and those you entered by hand.
  • The playlist. The files you or friends added, and for each one the name and device of who added it. Files from your music library are read in place, not copied.
  • The devices that connected, while receiving: for each phone or browser that used the remote control, the name and device it gave, its key, its IP address, when it was last seen, and whether you made it a favorite or blocked it.
  • Your music library, if you pick a folder: an index of its tags and covers, built on the device.
  • Your settings, including the page Murmur opens on, whether it receives, the audio format and the microphone options.

Everything is removed when you uninstall. The music folder you picked is yours and is never changed, except that files you choose to keep can be saved there.

What it sends

Only to the friend you choose, on your local network:

  • This device’s sound, while you send it: what apps play, including what Murmur itself plays or receives, captured with Android’s permission. Apps can opt out of being captured.
  • Your microphone, only if you turn on the Microphone section and send. While that section is open the microphone runs on the device to show its level, without sending anything.
  • Files, folders and library tracks you add to a friend’s playlist, with their titles and durations.
  • Your name, your device model and your public key, so the friend can show who added what and decide what you may do.

What a friend keeps is up to them: their Murmur remembers this device’s name, device, key and IP address, and keeps the files you sent in its playlist until they are removed.

What it receives

Receiving is off until you turn it on in the Receive page. Off, Murmur listens to nothing on the network. On, it listens on your local network for the receivers you have switched on:

  • Audio streams (VBAN, RTP, Scream, Snapcast, DLNA and UPnP, Bluetooth audio). It plays them; it does not record or store them.
  • Files sent with Murmur, a browser, LocalSend, KDE Connect, Quick Share (Linux) or Bluetooth. Accepted audio files go into the playlist.
  • The remote control: friends and browsers on the network can see the playlist, the sources and the volume, and, as you allow, change them.

To be found while receiving, Murmur announces its name, its network addresses and the ports it listens on to the local network (mDNS and SSDP). To find friends, it listens for those announcements. A DLNA app can ask Murmur to play a web address; Murmur then fetches it, which reveals your IP address to that site. With Snapcast on, it connects to the server you entered. Murmur makes no other network requests.

These are local network protocols. Audio streams and the remote control are not encrypted on the network. Requests are signed, so nobody can control a speaker as someone else, but anyone on the same network can hear a stream and see the playlist and the files sent to it. Use Murmur on networks you trust.

Who can join

While receiving, you choose in Remote access. In Open mode new phones and browsers join as guests, who can add files and remove their own. In Approval mode each one waits until you allow it. In Trust all mode anyone on the network gets full control. You can make a device a favorite, block it or forget it at any time. A shared QR code or link lets someone in for 24 hours, until you stop sharing.

Permissions

  • Screen and audio capture: Android asks each time you start sending this device’s sound, and shows that it is shared while it runs.
  • Microphone (record audio): to capture this device’s sound, and for the microphone only if you turn it on.
  • Camera: only while you scan a friend’s QR code. No image is stored or transmitted.
  • Internet, Wi-Fi state, Wi-Fi multicast and wake lock: to find friends, reach them, and receive on your network while something plays.
  • Notifications and foreground services: to show what plays, sends or uploads, and keep doing it with the screen off.
  • Bluetooth: only if you turn on Bluetooth audio or Bluetooth files.
  • Music and audio: only if you turn on Bluetooth files, to pick up audio files your phone’s Bluetooth app received.

Files, folders and the music library folder are chosen through the system’s pickers, so the app never gets access to your other files.

Your data, your control

Since we hold no personal data, there is nothing for us to access, correct or delete on your behalf. You can clear the playlist, forget friends and devices and turn receiving off in the app, or remove everything by uninstalling it. To have a friend’s Murmur forget you, ask them.

Children

Murmur is not directed at children under 13.
