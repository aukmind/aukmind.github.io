Tincan privacy policy

Last updated: 14 October 2026

Tincan is a purely peer-to-peer chat app. There is no Tincan server, no account and no sign-up. Messages travel directly between devices, encrypted end to end, and are stored only on those devices.

This policy describes what the app does. Where it says “we”, that means Aukmind, who publish Tincan.

What we collect

Nothing.We operate no server, no accounts and no analytics.

There is no advertising, no tracking, no telemetry and no crash reporting. Because nothing passes through us, a conversation only works while both devices are online, and we have no copy of anything to hand over, lose or leak.

What stays on your device

  • Your identity. A key pair generated on the device the first time you open the app. It is your address. Nobody issues it and nobody can look it up; you share it, or you don’t.
  • Your profile. Nickname, status, description and picture, if you set them.
  • Your conversations. Messages, and the photos and videos you send or receive.
  • Your contacts. The peers you accepted or blocked.
  • Your settings, including any relay of your own you configured.

All of it is encrypted on the device with a key held nowhere else. On first launch you choose what unlocks that key: your device’s biometrics or screen lock, a password only you know, or no lock at all.

  • A password cannot be recovered. Forgetting it means losing the messages, because there is no server holding a copy.
  • With no lock, the key is still kept in your device’s keystore rather than beside the files, so a copy of the storage cannot be read, but anyone who can open the device can.

The names of files someone sends you are the exception: they stay readable on disk even though the contents do not. While the app is open, decrypted copies of pictures and videos you view are written to a cache so they can be displayed; that cache is wiped every time the app starts. Everything is removed when you uninstall.

Backups you export are plain files under your control. The account backup contains your private key: anyone holding it can be you. Keep it as carefully as a password.

What leaves your device

Messages and media go to the peer you are talking to, encrypted end to end with WireGuard. Tincan prefers a direct connection between the two devices, which means the peer’s device learns your IP address, as with any direct connection. When a network blocks a direct path, the encrypted traffic is relayed instead.

Relays carry ciphertext they cannot read. A relay does see what any network intermediary sees: your IP address, the size and timing of traffic, and the fact that two addresses are talking. By default Tincan uses the public DERP relays operated by Tailscale, under their own terms. You can point the app at a relay you run yourself, in Settings.

Relay discovery. On first launch the app fetches the list of relays over HTTPS and measures the latency to each region to pick the nearest. This reveals your IP address to whoever serves that list and to the relays it measures. Setting your own relay avoids the default list.

Your profile is sent to peers you are connected to, so they see the name, status, description and picture you chose. Nothing else about you is transmitted.

Tincan makes no other network requests. Fonts and images are bundled in the app rather than fetched.

Who can reach you

Anyone who has your address can send you a chat request. You accept or refuse it, and a refusal stands until you undo it. Blocked peers are dropped silently; to them your device looks offline.

To become unreachable at an address you have shared, generate a new one. The old address stops working immediately and for good.

Permissions

  • Internet and network state: to reach your peers and notice when the network changes.
  • Camera: only while you are scanning a peer’s QR code. No image is stored or transmitted.
  • Notifications and background service: only if you turn on background receiving, and only to keep the connection alive for the time you chose, at most an hour.

Photos and videos you send are chosen through your system’s file picker, so the app never gets access to your library.

Your data, your control

We do not have, and cannot obtain, a way to read your messages. There is no key escrow and no recovery: that is a deliberate trade, and it is why losing a password loses the data. Since we hold no personal data, there is nothing for us to access, correct or delete on your behalf; you can do all of that directly in the app, or by uninstalling it.

Messages you send are stored on your peer’s device too, and what they do with them is under their control, not ours.

Children

Tincan is not directed at children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published on this page and with the app, dated at the top.
